What it is

Life, carried from our greenhouse to your soil

Greenfin is not a fertilizer and not a fish emulsion. It is the natural byproduct of a living ecosystem, bottled with the microbes still alive.

  1. Step one

    Living ecosystem

    Fish and plants share our aquaponics greenhouse. Fish manure is composted with worm and snail castings.

  2. Step two

    Living microbes

    That compost is rich in beneficial microorganisms. We bottle them alive, millions in every bottle.

  3. Step three

    Living soil

    In your soil they break down organic matter and turn locked-up nutrients into forms roots can take up.

Why growers use it

Healthy soil does the heavy lifting

Healthier soil structure

Active microbes break down organic matter and help build the crumbly, living soil that holds air and water.

Better nutrient uptake

Most nutrients in soil sit in forms plants can't use. Microbes convert them into forms roots can absorb.

Stronger roots and yield

Better nutrient absorption supports healthy root systems, and healthy roots support the harvest.

Gentle from seed to harvest

As an amendment rather than a concentrated fertilizer, it is very hard to overdo. Use it from germination to bloom.

Our story

Grown on the Pinckney Bend of the Missouri River

In 2017 we built a greenhouse from refurbished materials in New Haven, Missouri, about an hour west of St. Louis in the foothills of the Ozarks. Inside, fish and plants live in separate loops of a decoupled aquaponics system.

Greenfin is what that ecosystem gives back. Every bottle starts there.
