FAQ

Questions, answered plainly

What Greenfin is, how to use it, and the soil science behind it.

The product

What is Greenfin?

It's an all-natural soil amendment and conditioner containing millions of living beneficial microorganisms. It is made from composted fish manure with worm and snail castings from our aquaponics greenhouse in New Haven, Missouri. It is not a fish emulsion or a fish hydrolysate.

Is it a fertilizer?

No. It's an amendment. A fertilizer delivers nutrients directly; an amendment improves the soil so plants can use what is already there. That's also why we don't lead with an NPK number. Most of the nutrients in soil sit in non-available forms that roots can't absorb, and the microbes in Greenfin help release them into available forms.

What does "Life in a Bottle" on the label mean?

It's our tagline, and we mean it literally. Each bottle of Greenfin carries a living community of beneficial microbes from our greenhouse ecosystem. When you add it to your soil, you are adding life, not just ingredients.

How long does it keep?

Greenfin has a 24-month shelf life. Shake it well before each use.

Is it organic?

It's all-natural: composted fish manure and worm and snail castings, with nothing synthetic added. "Certified organic" is a separate legal label that requires a certification process, so rather than claim a label we describe Greenfin as all-natural.

Using it

What can I use it on?

Almost anything that grows: houseplants, gardens, lawns, golf courses, nurseries and farms, indoors or out. It works in every growing medium, including soil, coco, soilless mixes and hydro.

When can I use it?

Throughout the whole life cycle, from germination and cloning through vegetative growth to bloom. Each stage has its own rate.

How much do I use?

It depends on the stage, from 1–2 ml per gallon for germination to 12 oz per acre on turf. See the full application rates chart and the quick converter.

Can I use too much?

Because it is an amendment rather than a concentrated fertilizer, Greenfin is gentle and very hard to overdo. Even so, follow the rates chart. More isn't better, and the listed rates are what work in our greenhouse.

Can I use it in compost tea?

Yes. Adding Greenfin to a compost tea brings living beneficial microbes into the brew.

The science

What is the difference between an amendment and a fertilizer?

A fertilizer adds nutrients for the plant to take up now. An amendment improves the soil itself: its structure, its biology and its ability to hold and supply nutrients over time. Greenfin is an amendment that works by bringing soil biology back to life.

What are available and non-available nutrients?

Available nutrients are in a form roots can absorb, usually dissolved in soil water. Non-available nutrients are locked up in organic matter or bound to minerals. Soil microbes break down organic matter and transform those nutrients, moving them from the locked-up pool into the available one, which supports better nutrient uptake.

What do the beneficial bacteria actually do?

They decompose organic matter, drive the nitrogen cycle by converting nitrogen into forms plants can use, and help suppress disease-causing organisms by competing with them for space and food around the roots.

What is nitrification?

Nitrification is how ammonium becomes nitrate, the form of nitrogen most plants take up most easily. It happens in two steps. First, bacteria such as Nitrosomonas turn ammonium into nitrite. Then bacteria such as Nitrobacter turn nitrite into nitrate. The same process runs in our aquaponics biofilters, converting fish waste into plant food.

What is aquaponics?

Aquaponics raises fish and plants together. Fish waste is converted by bacteria into nutrients, the plants use those nutrients, and clean water returns to the fish. It is a small, working ecosystem.

What is a decoupled system?

In a decoupled aquaponics system the fish loop and the plant loop run separately, and water is moved between them when needed instead of flowing through both in one circuit. That lets each side run at the conditions that suit it best. Our greenhouse runs fully decoupled systems using deep water culture, ebb and flow, and aeroponics. See the diagram.

Why does soil biodiversity matter?

A diverse soil community has many organisms doing many jobs: recycling nutrients, building soil structure and keeping problem organisms in check. The more varied and active that community is, the more resilient the soil, and the better it supports healthy roots and plants.
